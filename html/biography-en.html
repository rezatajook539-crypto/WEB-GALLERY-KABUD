The Life of Vermeer

A quiet life in a small Dutch city, a large family, and a handful of paintings that outlived every trouble he faced.

Read the Timeline

A Life in Delft

1632

Born in Delft

Johannes Vermeer was baptized in Delft in October 1632. His father was a silk weaver who later ran an inn and dealt in paintings. No one knows for sure who taught him to paint.

1653

Marriage and the Guild

He married Catharina Bolnes and joined the Guild of St Luke, the painters' guild, which allowed him to work and sell as a master.

1662

Leader of the Guild

His fellow painters chose him as a guild leader in 1662 and again in 1670, a sign of the respect he had in Delft.

1675

Death at 43

He died suddenly in December 1675 and was buried in Delft's Oude Kerk.

Where His Paintings Came From

01

His Hometown

Delft's tidy houses, canals and window light were his everyday world, and they shaped the calm rooms he painted.

02

Other Painters

He looked at Dutch masters of home life such as Gerard ter Borch and Pieter de Hooch. A painting by Dirck van Baburen, owned by his mother-in-law, even appears on the wall in two of his interiors.

03

Science and Maps

Delft was a city of lenses, maps and instruments. Maps hang in his rooms, and the microscope pioneer Antonie van Leeuwenhoek was named to oversee his estate after his death.

04

Faith

He is thought to have become Catholic around his marriage, in a mostly Protestant country. His Allegory of Faith is one of his few openly religious paintings.

Hard Times

1654

The Delft Thunderclap

A huge explosion of a gunpowder store destroyed part of Delft in October 1654 and killed many people, among them the painter Carel Fabritius.

1660s

A Big Family

He and Catharina had around fifteen children (sources differ on the exact number), and the family lived in his mother-in-law's house. Raising them on the income of a slow painter and art dealer was never easy.

1672

The Year of Disaster

France invaded the Dutch Republic and the art market collapsed. Vermeer struggled to sell his paintings, as well as works by other artists that he dealt in. A collector believed to have bought many of his paintings died in 1674.

1675

Debt and an Early Death

He died within days of falling ill, leaving his widow and eleven children, eight of them still minors, with heavy debts. Catharina later wrote that the war and his worries had broken him.

1860s

Forgotten, Then Found

For nearly two centuries his name almost vanished, until the French critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger brought him back to public attention.

No Words, Only Paintings

Vermeer left no letters, diary or notes about his ideas. What people call his philosophy is what art historians read in his paintings, so these ideas are interpretations, not his own words.

Explore Technique

Four Ideas Behind the Silence

01

Look Closely

He gave full attention to small things: a pearl, a loaf of bread, a letter, a ray of light.

02

Stillness

His figures pause. The silence and calm invite the viewer to slow down too.

03

Dignity in Everyday Life

A maid pouring milk gets the same care as a grand subject. Ordinary work and ordinary people deserve a masterpiece.

04

Balance and Restraint

Few objects, careful space and soft light. In Woman Holding a Balance, the scales seem to be empty, perhaps a reminder to live with moderation.
